这是一篇带时间戳的预测。

2026 年，Agent 的重心明显向更广泛的人群移动。年初最醒目的产品是 Coding Agent；年中，办公 Agent 开始争夺白领工作流；到下半年，Personal Agent 逐渐成为新的入口想象。

如果只看产品名字，这像是三个彼此竞争的品类。换一个尺度看，它们更像同一条采用路径：

`Professional → Prosumer → Public`

Agent 先服务能够容忍复杂度、也能判断结果的专业开发者，再进入有明确工作价值的办公人群，最后尝试成为普通人的日常基础设施。

## 2026 年的三次重心移动

我在原始推演里把 2026 年粗略分成三个阶段：

1. **1—4 月：Coding Agent。** Claude Code 等产品首先让专业开发者感受到 Agent 不只是回答问题，而是可以进入生产过程。
2. **5—8 月：办公 Agent。** ChatGPT Work、Claude Cowork、WorkBuddy 等尝试把 Agent 带进更普遍的知识工作。
3. **9—12 月：Personal Agent。** 产品开始争夺人的长期 Context、跨任务协作，以及支付、通知和日常行动入口。

月份不必被理解得太精确。日历并不是特别可靠的产品经理。真正重要的是采用人群在扩大，而每扩大一层，产品需要解决的问题都会改变。

## Personal Agent 会撞上基础设施边界

Personal Agent 的想象很完整：理解一个人、跨越多个服务、代表他完成行动。但现实里的平台、支付与身份体系并不是为一个通用 Agent 准备的。

电商平台可能限制自动访问，支付系统会保留自己的控制边界，大型生态也倾向于发展自己的入口。一个 Agent 即使理解用户，也未必拥有完成行动所需的权限。

这意味着 Personal Agent 可能不会收敛成一个包办生活的超级入口。它更可能在不同场景中分化，并针对不同目标形成更深的产品形态。

## 三条可能的分化路径

### Work：重新组织人与 Agent 的协作

下一轮办公 Agent 竞争不会只是谁能生成更多文档，而是谁能重新设计任务怎样被拆分、判断怎样被保留、责任怎样被确认。

给每个人配一个相同的 Agent，并不一定是高效的组织方式。更值得探索的问题是：哪些工作应该由 Agent 持续完成，人在什么节点提供 Context、判断、纠正和承担责任？

### Leisure：不是闲着，而是自我发展

这里的 Leisure 不是消磨时间，更接近古典意义上的 `scholē`：摆脱谋生劳动以后，用于学习、思考、自我培养和形成能力的时间。

这类 Agent 不以替用户完成更多任务为唯一目标。它可能帮助人读懂复杂材料、保持长期练习、整理经验，或者把一段模糊兴趣发展成真正的能力。

### Entertainment：把参与本身变成价值

娱乐并不只是给工作 Agent 加上动画和积分。它优化的是另一件事：人为什么愿意回来、愿意持续参与，以及产品如何提供情绪价值。

Duolingo 是一个有用的提醒。用户表面上在学习语言，但连续记录、角色、反馈节奏和轻微戏剧性同样构成产品。未来的 Entertainment Agent 可能并不让人“更像 AI Native”，而是让人与 AI 相处这件事本身变得有趣。

## 关于 2027 年的基准判断

我的基准情景是：办公 Agent 会率先重新竞争；面向自我发展的 Agent 随后获得更清晰的产品形态；娱乐化则会把 Agent 带向更广泛的人群。

我不会为具体月份下注。这个预测真正关心的是顺序背后的原因：生产价值最容易被衡量，自我发展需要更长期的 Context，而娱乐最终会把使用门槛降到最低。

## 什么会证明这个判断错了

这项推演至少有三条可以被观察的失败路径：

- 平台、支付与身份权限快速标准化，让一个通用 Personal Agent 能顺畅完成跨服务行动；
- 用户长期把工作、自我发展和娱乐交给同一个产品，没有出现明显的使用分化；
- 大众用户只接受 Agent 作为工作工具，不愿让它进入更长期、更私人的生活关系。

如果这些情况发生，分化可能比我预想得更弱。但如果通用入口持续受到基础设施和信任边界限制，Work、Leisure 与 Entertainment 就不只是三个营销标签，而会成为三种真正不同的产品逻辑。

This is a timestamped forecast.

Across 2026, the center of gravity for agents moved toward a broader public. Coding agents defined the beginning of the year. Office agents then competed for white-collar workflows. By the second half, the personal agent had become the next imagined interface.

Seen as product categories, these look like separate contests. Seen from farther away, they form one adoption path:

`Professional → Prosumer → Public`

Agents first served developers who could tolerate complexity and judge the output. They then moved into office work with visible economic value. Now they are trying to become part of ordinary life.

## Three shifts during 2026

In the original forecast, I divided 2026 into three rough periods:

1. **January to April: coding agents.** Products such as Claude Code made it clear that an agent could enter production rather than merely answer questions.
2. **May to August: office agents.** ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork, WorkBuddy, and similar products tried to carry that behavior into more general knowledge work.
3. **September to December: personal agents.** Products began competing for long-term Context, cross-task continuity, and access to notifications, payments, and everyday action.

The months should not be taken too literally. Calendars are terrible product managers. The meaningful shift is the widening user base: every new group changes what the product must be good at.

## Personal agents will meet infrastructure boundaries

The personal-agent promise is expansive: understand a person, move across services, and act on their behalf. The platform, payment, and identity layers underneath that promise were not designed for a universal agent.

Commerce platforms may restrict automated access. Payment systems preserve their own control boundaries. Large ecosystems have incentives to build their own entry points. An agent may understand the user while still lacking permission to complete the action.

That pressure makes one universal assistant less inevitable. Personal agents may instead deepen around different situations and optimize for different kinds of value.

## Three possible directions

### Work: reorganizing human–agent collaboration

The next office-agent contest will not be won by generating more documents. The deeper opportunity is to redesign how tasks are divided, where judgment remains human, and how responsibility is confirmed.

Giving every person the same assistant may be less useful than asking a harder question: what should an agent do continuously, and where should a person provide Context, correction, judgment, and accountability?

### Leisure: not idleness, but self-development

Here, leisure is closer to the classical idea of `scholē` than to killing time. It is the time left after necessary labor for learning, reflection, self-cultivation, and building capability.

A leisure agent would not measure success only by tasks completed. It might help someone understand difficult material, sustain a practice, organize lived experience, or turn a vague interest into a real ability.

### Entertainment: making participation itself valuable

Entertainment is not an office agent with points and animation. It optimizes for a different question: why does someone return, continue, and feel something while using the product?

Duolingo is a useful reminder. The user is ostensibly learning a language, but streaks, characters, feedback rhythm, and mild theatricality are also the product. An entertainment agent may not make people more “AI native.” It may simply make living with AI enjoyable.

## A baseline view of 2027

My baseline scenario is that office agents compete first, agents for self-development then acquire clearer product forms, and entertainment eventually carries agents to a broader public.

I would not bet on the exact month. The sequence matters more than the calendar: production value is easiest to measure, self-development needs longer Context, and entertainment can lower the threshold for continued use.

## What would prove this forecast wrong

At least three observable developments would weaken the argument:

- platforms, payments, and identity permissions standardize quickly enough for one personal agent to act smoothly across services;
- users keep work, self-development, and entertainment inside one product without meaningful behavioral separation;
- the public accepts agents as work tools but refuses longer, more personal relationships with them.

If those conditions emerge, the split may be weaker than I expect. If infrastructure and trust continue to constrain the universal assistant, however, work, leisure, and entertainment will become more than market labels. They will become different product logics.
