[上一篇文章](/alexliu.readme/writing/ai-applications-will-survive/)里，我讨论了为什么 AI 应用仍然有机会。

模型会继续吸收今天的功能，但基础设施不会自动发明属于 AI 时代的产品。真正重要的应用，需要进入人的工作和生活，改变人的行为，最后形成新的产品形态。

最近 Personal Agent 开始进入支付、通知和跨服务操作。这意味着，Agent 正在从“完成一个任务”走向“长期理解一个人，并代表他采取行动”。

如果继续沿着这个方向推演，2027 年可能会出现三个依次展开的方向：Work、Lifestyle 和 Entertainment。

它们不是三个彼此独立的市场。后一个方向，很可能是前一个方向带来的结果。

## Agent 正在从专业人群走向大众

如果粗略回顾 2026 年，可以看到三次重心转移。

年初最受关注的是 Coding Agent，Claude Code 是其中最有代表性的产品。它让开发者第一次明显感受到，Agent 不只是回答问题，也可以进入真实的生产流程。

年中，竞争开始转向办公场景。ChatGPT Work、Claude Cowork、WorkBuddy 等产品尝试把类似的能力带进更广泛的知识工作。

到了下半年，Personal Agent 开始受到关注。产品不再只想完成一次任务，而是希望掌握长期 Context，连接通知、支付和不同服务，并逐渐成为用户的日常入口。

这背后是一条相对清晰的扩散路径：

`Professional → Prosumer → Public`

Agent 先进入专业开发者的生产流程，再进入有明确工作价值的办公场景，最后走向普通用户。

但 Personal Agent 真正走向大众时，会遇到一个基础设施问题。

一个 Agent 可以帮用户寻找商品，但电商平台未必愿意开放完整的访问权限。它可以理解用户的支付意图，但支付宝、微信支付等系统仍然会保留自己的安全边界和用户关系。大型平台也更可能发展自己的 Agent，而不是把入口交给外部产品。

因此，Personal Agent 可能出现一种尴尬状态：它知道很多信息，也可以完成一部分操作，却经常停在最后一步之前。

在支付、身份和平台权限尚未完全开放的情况下，Personal Agent 很难迅速成为一个包办所有生活场景的超级入口。Agent 的竞争很可能因此重新回到最容易衡量价值的领域：Work。

## Work：从“给人配 Agent”到“给 Agent 配人”

过去比较常见的思路，是先找到一个专业的人，再为他配置合适的 Agent。

比如，公司已经有一位 Marketing 专业人员，于是为他寻找 Marketing Agent，帮助他搜索信息、生成内容、分析数据和执行任务。

在这种模式里，人仍然是工作的中心。Agent 的作用是提高这个人的效率。

但未来可能出现相反的情况。

市场上已经有人做出了能力非常强的 Marketing Agent。它掌握行业信息，可以持续分析内容、生成方案、追踪数据，甚至比普通 Marketing 从业者更熟悉某些专业流程。

公司此时需要思考的，就不再是“应该给 Marketing 配什么 Agent”，而是：

> 这个 Marketing Agent 还需要什么样的人，才能真正完成业务闭环？

即使 Agent 比人更擅长执行专业任务，它仍然无法独立完成所有工作。

它需要有人判断产出的内容到底好不好，有人把控整体品牌调性，也需要有人与 KOL、KOC 沟通，协商预算、合作方式和具体细节。出现争议或异常情况时，也仍然需要人承担责任并做出最后决定。

未来的人类工作，可能会越来越集中在这些闭环上：

- 判断结果是否可用；
- 把控品牌、审美与整体方向；
- 处理人与人之间的沟通和信任；
- 面对例外情况并承担责任。

这不是简单地让人给 Agent 打工，而是工作的组织中心发生了变化。

过去，公司先定义人的岗位，再寻找工具帮助他。未来，公司可能先获得一个能力非常强的 Agent，再围绕它仍然无法独立完成的部分配置合适的人。

这种变化会直接影响人对自身能力的理解。

## Leisure 与 Lifestyle：人为什么需要自我提升

当公司从“给专业人配 Agent”转向“给 Agent 配合适的人”以后，普通的专业执行能力会逐渐失去一部分价值。

如果 Marketing Agent 已经比很多人更擅长生成内容、分析数据和制定计划，那么人需要证明的，就不再只是自己会不会完成这些任务。

他需要证明自己具备 Agent 仍然缺少的能力：

- 能否判断什么是好的结果；
- 能否建立稳定的审美和品牌意识；
- 能否理解复杂的人际关系；
- 能否在不确定情况下做出决定；
- 能否承担结果和责任。

这会制造一种非常强烈的自我提升需求。

人需要不断提高自己的判断力、表达能力、审美、沟通方式和对世界的理解，才能继续成为那个“适合与 Agent 一起工作的人”。

这里的 Leisure 是人产生的需求，Lifestyle Agent 则是承接这种需求的产品形态。

Leisure 不只是管理日程、记录饮食或提醒运动，也不只是传统意义上的“休闲”。从《尼各马可伦理学》的角度来看，闲暇并不只是休息，而是人在摆脱谋生劳动以后，用于学习、沉思、自我完善和培养德性的时间。

当大量执行工作被 Agent 接管以后，人会获得更多时间，同时也会面临更强的压力：我应该怎样使用这些时间？我应该提升什么能力？我怎样才能不被新的工作系统淘汰？

Lifestyle Agent 可能帮助人阅读、学习、反思、练习表达、整理经验，并逐渐形成自己的判断方式。

它的核心价值不是替用户完成更多任务，而是帮助用户成为一个更有能力的人。

## Entertainment：真实提升很难，提供提升感更容易

问题在于，真正提升一个人的能力非常困难。

判断力、审美、表达能力和人际沟通，都不可能通过几次对话迅速建立。它们需要长期练习、真实反馈和持续反思。

如果产品真的承诺帮助用户提高能力，它需要长期理解用户，设计有效的练习，提供准确反馈，还要面对一个事实：用户的进步可能非常缓慢，而且很难被立即感知。

相比之下，让用户感觉自己正在进步，要容易得多。

产品可以提供连续记录、等级、分数、角色反馈和阶段总结，让用户持续获得“我正在变好”的感觉。用户未必真的建立了更强的判断力，但他可以清楚看到自己的使用天数、完成次数和能力标签。

Duolingo 就展示了这种逻辑。

用户当然是在学习语言，但连续记录、角色、反馈节奏和游戏化体验同样构成了产品。它不仅帮助用户学习，也让用户持续感受到自己仍然在学习。

因此，Lifestyle Agent 很可能进一步走向 Entertainment。

最开始，很多产品团队会倾向于做真正能够提升用户能力、提高工作效率的产品。但很快，他们通常会遇到两重限制：一方面，多数团队很难把长期训练和反馈系统做得足够好，真正帮助用户持续提升；另一方面，他们也会发现，结构性的自我提升需求并不等于用户愿意接受长期、高强度的训练。不少用户反而更希望从产品中获得安全感、满足感和即时反馈。

于是，产品团队和用户逐渐形成一种默契：团队不再执着于提供真正提升能力的工具，用户也不再追求显著的效率提升。产品最终转向反馈感、参与感和情绪价值——本质上是 Entertainment，只是被包装成一种轻松、舒适的 Lifestyle。

因此，关键并不只是产品是否让人感到自己变得更好了，而在于这种提升究竟来自真实能力的增长，还是来自一种经过精心设计的提升感。两者有时会同时存在，但它们并不是同一件事。

## 为什么顺序可能是 Work、Lifestyle、Entertainment

Work 最先出现，是因为它的价值最容易衡量。一个 Agent 是否提高生产效率、减少成本或完成更多任务，公司可以直接判断。

Work Agent 改变人与 Agent 的分工以后，人会产生新的自我提升需求，因此 Lifestyle Agent 随之出现。

而当产品团队真正开始解决“怎样提升一个人”时，又会发现这件事非常困难。相比真实改变用户，让用户愿意持续使用，并感受到自己正在改变，是一条更容易成立的产品路径。

Entertainment 因此排在最后。

具体的时间点可能会发生变化，三个方向也可能重叠出现。但它们之间存在一条相对清晰的因果关系：

1. Work Agent 改变人与 Agent 的分工；
2. 新的分工制造更强的自我提升需求；
3. Lifestyle Agent 尝试帮助人提升能力；
4. 真实提升困难、缓慢，而且难以衡量；
5. 产品逐渐转向参与感、反馈感和情绪价值；
6. Entertainment Agent 随之出现。

这可能是 Personal Agent 之后，更值得关注的一条演进路径。

In [the previous essay](/alexliu.readme/writing/ai-applications-will-survive/?lang=en), I argued that AI applications still have room to grow.

Models will keep absorbing today's features, but infrastructure does not automatically invent the products of an era. Important applications have to enter real work and life, change behavior, and eventually create new product forms.

Personal agents are now moving into payments, notifications, and actions across services. The ambition is no longer to finish one task, but to understand a person over time and act on their behalf.

If that direction continues, three areas may unfold in sequence during 2027: work, lifestyle, and entertainment.

They are not three unrelated markets. Each may be produced by the one before it.

## Agents are moving from professionals to the public

A rough view of 2026 shows three shifts.

Coding agents dominated the beginning of the year. Claude Code made it obvious that an agent could enter production rather than merely answer questions.

The contest then moved into office work. ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork, WorkBuddy, and similar products tried to carry the same behavior into broader knowledge work.

By the second half of the year, personal agents had become the new focus. Products began competing for long-term Context, notifications, payments, and a place in everyday action rather than one isolated task.

Beneath those shifts is one adoption path:

`Professional → Prosumer → Public`

Agents first entered production through developers, moved into office work with measurable value, and then began reaching the public.

But when personal agents reach the public, they run into an infrastructure problem.

An agent may find products for a user, but a commerce platform may not grant it complete access. It may understand the user's intent to pay, while Alipay and WeChat Pay preserve their own security boundaries and customer relationships. Large platforms are also more likely to build their own agents than surrender the entry point to an external product.

The result may be a personal agent that knows a great deal and can complete part of an action, yet repeatedly stops one step before the end.

As long as payments, identity, and platform permissions remain fragmented, the personal agent will struggle to become one universal entrance to life. Competition may therefore return to the area where value is easiest to measure: work.

## Work: from assigning an agent to a person to assigning people to an agent

The familiar approach begins with a professional and gives them a suitable agent.

A company already has a marketer, for example, so it finds a marketing agent to help with research, content generation, analysis, and execution. The person remains the center of the work; the agent improves that person's efficiency.

The future may reverse the arrangement.

Suppose the market already offers a very strong marketing agent. It follows industry information, analyzes content continuously, generates plans, tracks performance, and may understand parts of the specialist workflow better than an ordinary marketer.

The company's question is no longer “Which agent should we give our marketer?” It becomes:

> What kind of person does this marketing agent still need in order to close the business loop?

Even an agent that is better at specialist execution cannot finish everything alone.

Someone still has to judge whether the output is good, preserve the overall brand tone, and negotiate budgets, formats, and details with KOLs and KOCs. When an exception or dispute appears, a person still has to make the final call and carry responsibility.

Human work may increasingly concentrate around those loops:

- judging whether an output is usable;
- preserving brand, taste, and direction;
- handling communication and trust between people;
- responding to exceptions and carrying accountability.

This is not simply making people work for agents. It is a change in the organizing center of work.

Companies used to define the human role first and then find tools to assist it. They may instead acquire a capable agent and organize people around what it still cannot do independently.

That change will affect how people understand their own value.

## Leisure and lifestyle: why people will need self-development

When companies move from assigning agents to professionals toward assigning suitable people around agents, ordinary specialist execution loses some of its value.

If a marketing agent already generates content, analyzes data, and produces plans better than many people, the person has to demonstrate something beyond executing those tasks.

They have to show that they can judge a good result, develop taste and brand awareness, understand difficult human relationships, decide under uncertainty, and carry responsibility for the outcome.

That creates a strong demand for self-development.

People have to improve judgment, expression, taste, communication, and their understanding of the world if they want to remain the kind of person who can work well with an agent.

Leisure is the human need; the lifestyle agent is the product form that may respond to it.

From the perspective of the *Nicomachean Ethics*, leisure is not merely rest. It is the time beyond necessary labor that can be used for learning, contemplation, self-improvement, and cultivating virtue.

As agents take over more execution, people may gain more time while feeling greater pressure: How should I use that time? What should I improve? How do I avoid becoming irrelevant to the new system of work?

A lifestyle agent may help someone read, learn, reflect, practise expression, organize experience, and gradually develop a way of judging the world.

Its central value is not completing more tasks for the user. It is helping the user become more capable.

## Entertainment: real improvement is hard; the feeling of improvement is easier

The difficulty is that genuinely improving a person is hard.

Judgment, taste, expression, and interpersonal skill cannot be built through a few conversations. They require long practice, real feedback, and sustained reflection.

A product that promises actual improvement has to understand the user over time, design effective practice, provide accurate feedback, and accept that progress may be slow and difficult to perceive.

Making users feel that they are improving is much easier.

A product can offer streaks, levels, scores, character feedback, and progress summaries. The user may not have developed stronger judgment, but they can clearly see days used, exercises completed, and ability labels earned.

Duolingo demonstrates this logic. People are learning a language, but streaks, characters, feedback rhythms, and game mechanics are also part of the product. It helps people learn while continually reminding them that they are still learning.

That is why lifestyle agents may drift toward entertainment.

Many teams begin by wanting to build products that genuinely improve capability and working efficiency. They soon meet two limits. First, most teams struggle to build a long-term training and feedback system good enough to produce sustained improvement. Second, a structural demand for self-development does not mean users are willing to endure long, demanding practice. Many would rather receive safety, satisfaction, and immediate feedback.

The team and the user gradually reach an accommodation. The team stops insisting on genuine capability improvement, and the user stops demanding a dramatic increase in efficiency. The product moves toward feedback, participation, and emotional value—entertainment packaged as an easier and more comfortable lifestyle product.

The important distinction is therefore not simply whether users feel better. It is whether that improvement comes from actual capability growth or from a carefully designed feeling of progress. The two may coexist, but they are not the same.

## Why the order may be work, lifestyle, and entertainment

Work comes first because its value is easiest to measure. A company can directly observe whether an agent increases output, reduces cost, or completes more work.

When work agents change the division of labor, people develop a new need for self-improvement, creating the conditions for lifestyle agents.

Once teams attempt to improve a person, they discover how difficult the task is. Sustained use and a visible feeling of change are easier product outcomes than genuine transformation.

Entertainment therefore comes later.

The timing may change, and the three areas may overlap. Their causal relationship is still relatively clear:

1. Work agents change the division of labor between people and agents.
2. The new division creates stronger demand for self-development.
3. Lifestyle agents attempt to improve human capability.
4. Real improvement proves slow, difficult, and hard to measure.
5. Products shift toward participation, feedback, and emotional value.
6. Entertainment agents emerge.

This may be the more important trajectory to watch after the personal agent.
