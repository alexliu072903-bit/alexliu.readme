[上一篇文章](/alexliu.readme/writing/ai-applications-will-survive/)里，我说 AI 应用仍然有很大的机会。关键不是再给模型套一层 UI，而是进入人的生活，影响人的行为，最后重新组织人怎样工作和生活。

当时我用了一个有点奇怪的比喻：我们已经有了 AI 时代的电力，却还没有发明它的冰箱。

最近 Personal Agent 开始往支付、通知和跨服务行动里走。这至少说明，“冰箱”这件事有人真的开始想了。今天我想再往前推一年：如果 2026 年最后一场大战是 Personal Agent，2027 年会发生什么？

我的判断是，它不会继续收敛成一个越来越大的万能助手。它会分成三条路：**Work、Leisure 和 Entertainment。**

## Agent 正在从专业人群走向大众

如果硬要把 2026 年按月份切开，我会这样分：

- 1—4 月是 Coding Agent，代表是 Claude Code。
- 5—8 月是办公 Agent，ChatGPT Work、Claude Cowork、WorkBuddy 都在争夺知识工作。
- 9—12 月是 Personal Agent。产品不只想完成一个任务，而是开始争夺长期 Context、通知、支付和日常行动入口。

这三个阶段背后，其实是一条很简单的路径：

`Professional → Prosumer → Public`

Agent 先进入专业开发者的生产流程，再进入有明确价值的办公场景，最后走向普通人。

月份大概率不会像我写得这么整齐。行业不会等到 1 月 1 日，再礼貌地切换下一场战争。但方向是清楚的：使用 Agent 的人越来越多，产品要回答的问题也会随之改变。

## Personal Agent 可能会卡在半路

Personal Agent 最完整的想象，是理解一个人、跨越多个服务，再代表他完成行动。问题是，今天的互联网基础设施并不是为这件事准备的。

假设一个 Agent 想替你购物，Amazon 未必愿意让它自由进入；它想替你付钱，Alipay、WeChat Pay 也更可能发展自己的入口。每个平台都愿意让 Agent 带来交易，但未必愿意把用户关系和最后的控制权交出去。

结果可能是：Personal Agent 什么都知道一点，也什么都能碰一下，但始终停在“最后一步之前”。不上不下。

这不是模型能力的问题，而是基础设施、权限和利益的问题。也正因为通用入口很难一次吃掉全部生活，Agent 才可能在 2027 年开始明显分化。

## 第一条路：Work

办公 Agent 还会再打一轮，而且我觉得问题会从“能力”转向“思路”。

过去比较常见的想法，是给每个人配一个合适的 Agent。这个思路很自然，但也可能非常低效，因为它默认人的岗位、流程和组织方式不需要改变，只需要给旧位置增加一个新工具。

更激进的做法是反过来：**不是给人配 Agent，而是围绕 Agent 已经能做的事情，重新安排人。**

这句话听起来有一点冒犯，好像人在给 Agent 打工。但我真正想说的是，组织需要重新决定：哪些任务由 Agent 持续完成，哪些地方必须由人提供 Context、做判断、纠正错误并承担责任。就像工厂拥有独立电机之后，改变的不只是动力来源，还有厂房布局。

## 第二条路：Leisure

这里的 Leisure 不是“休闲一下”，也不是躺在沙发上什么都不做。

我用的是《尼各马可伦理学》更接近的意思：人在摆脱谋生劳动以后，用于学习、沉思、自我培养和形成德性的积极状态。

如果 Work Agent 让人完成工作，那么 Leisure Agent 处理的是另一个问题：人应该怎样变得更有能力？它可能陪一个人读懂复杂材料、保持长期练习、整理经历，或者把模糊的兴趣发展成真正的技能。

当然，这里也很可能出现新一轮“启动启动启动”。但至少，它和替人写一封邮件不是同一种产品。

## 第三条路：Entertainment

Entertainment 会是 Leisure 的自然延伸，因为真正提升一个人很难，让一个人愿意每天回来则相对容易。

Duolingo 是个很好的例子。人们表面上在学习语言，但连续记录、角色、反馈节奏和轻微的戏剧性，同样构成产品。它没有只问“怎样让用户学得更快”，还在问“怎样让用户明天继续回来”。

所以我怀疑，很多面向大众的 Agent 最后会追求 Emotional Value。相较于真的提升一个人使用 Agent 和理解 AI 的水平，让人相信自己正在变得更 AI Native，可能是一条更短的路。

这听起来有点讽刺，但娱乐化几乎不可避免。再强的能力，如果没有人愿意持续使用，也很难变成大众产品。

## 我真正下注的不是月份

如果一定要给 2027 年排一个顺序，我会猜：年初办公 Agent 再次开战，年中出现更清楚的 Leisure Agent，年底则轮到 Entertainment Agent。

这个时间表很可能是错的。我真正下注的是分化本身。

如果支付、身份和平台权限突然变得非常开放，一个通用 Personal Agent 当然可能继续扩张；如果用户愿意把工作、成长和娱乐都交给同一个入口，三条路也可能重新合在一起。

但只要基础设施仍然分裂，不同场景衡量价值的方式也不同，Work、Leisure 和 Entertainment 就不会只是三个营销词。它们会成为三种产品。

如果真走到这一步，做 World Model 和 Multimodal 的公司大概会很开心。至于它们要不要付我一笔咨询费，是另一件事。

In [the previous essay](/alexliu.readme/writing/ai-applications-will-survive/?lang=en), I argued that AI applications still have enormous room to grow. The opportunity is not another interface wrapped around a model. It is entering real life, changing behavior, and eventually reorganizing how people work and live.

I used a slightly strange line to make the point: we already have the electricity of the AI era, but we have not invented its refrigerator.

Personal agents are now beginning to touch payments, notifications, and actions across services. At the very least, someone is trying to design the refrigerator. So I want to push the argument one year forward: if the last major agent contest of 2026 is the personal agent, what happens in 2027?

My guess is that the market does not converge on one ever-larger universal assistant. It splits three ways: **work, leisure, and entertainment.**

## From professionals to the public

If I force 2026 into a calendar, it looks roughly like this:

- January to April belonged to coding agents, with Claude Code as the clearest marker.
- May to August belonged to office agents, as ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork, WorkBuddy, and others competed for knowledge work.
- September to December belongs to the personal agent: long-term Context, notifications, payments, and everyday action rather than one isolated task.

Beneath those categories is one simple adoption path:

`Professional → Prosumer → Public`

Agents entered the production process through developers, moved into office work with legible economic value, and are now trying to reach ordinary life.

The months will almost certainly refuse to behave this neatly. Industries do not wait for January 1 before politely beginning the next war. The direction matters more: as the audience widens, the product has to become good at something different.

## The personal agent may get stuck halfway

The complete personal-agent dream is to understand a person, move across services, and act on their behalf. The internet underneath that dream was not built for it.

If an agent wants to shop for you, Amazon may not want it wandering freely through the store. If it wants to pay for you, Alipay and WeChat Pay have every reason to build their own entry points. Platforms welcome transactions; they are less enthusiastic about surrendering the customer relationship and the final moment of control.

The result may be an agent that knows a little about everything and can touch almost every service, yet keeps stopping one step before completion. Neither useless nor fully useful.

That is not only a model problem. It is an infrastructure, permission, and incentive problem. And because one universal assistant cannot easily swallow the whole of life, the market may begin to split.

## Path one: work

Office agents will fight another round. This time, I suspect the bottleneck will be the idea rather than the capability.

The common approach is to find the right agent for every employee. It sounds sensible, but it may be deeply inefficient because it preserves the job, workflow, and organization, then attaches a new tool to the old position.

The deliberately rude version of the alternative is: **stop finding an agent for every person; start finding the people an agent deserves.**

I do not mean turning humans into accessories. I mean redesigning the division of work: what should an agent do continuously, and where must a person provide Context, judgment, correction, and accountability? Once factories had independent electric motors, they did not merely change the source of power. They changed the floor plan.

## Path two: leisure

Leisure here does not mean idleness or an empty afternoon.

I mean something closer to the classical idea of `scholē`: the active time beyond necessary labor used for learning, contemplation, self-cultivation, and becoming more capable.

If a work agent helps someone finish work, a leisure agent asks a different question: how does this person become better? It might help someone understand difficult material, sustain a practice, organize experience, or turn a vague interest into an actual skill.

There will, of course, be another round of companies shouting “launch, launch, launch.” Still, this is not the same product as an agent that writes one more email.

## Path three: entertainment

Entertainment follows naturally because genuinely improving a person is hard; giving them a reason to return tomorrow is easier.

Duolingo is a useful example. People ostensibly go there to learn a language, but streaks, characters, feedback rhythm, and mild theatricality are also the product. It does not ask only how users learn faster. It asks how they come back.

I suspect many public-facing agents will eventually optimize for emotional value. It may be easier to make someone feel more AI native than to genuinely improve their ability to use agents and understand AI.

That sounds cynical, but entertainment is difficult to avoid. Capability without continued use rarely becomes a mass-market product.

## I am betting on the split, not the month

If I had to place the three contests on a 2027 calendar, I would put office agents first, clearer leisure agents in the middle, and entertainment agents later in the year.

The schedule will probably be wrong. The split is the actual bet.

If payments, identity, and platform permissions suddenly become open, one general personal agent may keep expanding. If people happily give work, growth, and entertainment to the same interface, the paths may merge again.

But as long as the infrastructure remains fragmented and each situation measures value differently, work, leisure, and entertainment will become more than three market labels. They will become three products.

If that happens, the world-model and multimodal companies should be delighted. Whether any of them owe me a consulting fee is a separate question.
