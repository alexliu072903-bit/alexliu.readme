# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site serves two primary audiences:

1. Potential employers, founders, product leaders, and collaborators evaluating whether Alex can turn ambiguous AI-product questions into clear, working outcomes.
2. Social-media readers and AI beginners who want a deeper, more durable view of Alex's projects and thinking than a chronological feed can provide.

They should be able to scan quickly without being treated as intellectually unsophisticated. Technical complexity may be hidden; the reasoning behind a product should remain legible.

## Product Purpose

This is Alex Liu's independent, durable personal website. It is the public source of truth for what he has made, what he has written, and who he is.

The site should never directly claim that Alex is rational, tasteful, imaginative, patient, or unusually capable. It should arrange enough honest evidence that visitors reach that conclusion themselves.

The internal narrative is:

> Alex can remain imaginative in uncertainty, then use reason, restraint, and practice to turn part of that uncertainty into reality.

Three tensions organize the work:

- imagination and certainty;
- exploration and restraint;
- reason and the "push of fate" that emerges from repeated real-world signals.

Success means a visitor can describe Alex as someone who enters unfamiliar problems, builds an understanding, makes something real, acknowledges its limits, and leaves behind a reusable product, protocol, or Skill.

## Brand Personality

Lucid · exploratory · restrained.

The voice is calm but not timid, ambitious but not inflated, technically literate without using technology as spectacle. It should feel like a working mind rather than a polished founder mythology: observant, unfinished in an honest way, and precise about what is known.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI enthusiast portfolios and "future of AI" storytelling.
- Startup-bro confidence, exaggerated founder narratives, and technical overclaiming.
- Cyberpunk, neon, glassmorphism, purple gradients, particle fields, and decorative AI futurism.
- Beige editorial templates with display-serif headlines, tiny monospace labels, and ruled three-column grids.
- Identical project cards, resume websites, feature-list case studies, and fabricated metrics.
- Company work, private source code, internal screenshots, or unverified product status presented as public personal work.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence, not adjectives.** Let artifacts, decisions, links, and honest status create the impression.
2. **Exploration held by structure.** Permit unusual composition and motion, but give every deviation a stable grid, hierarchy, and reading order.
3. **Current state is part of the story.** Historical, private-source, experimental, and discontinued work must be named plainly.
4. **Less, but not safer.** Remove anything without meaning; do not confuse restraint with generic minimalism.
5. **Motion carries meaning.** Motion may indicate emergence, continuity, branching, or resolution. Decorative looping is forbidden.
6. **User-owned context.** Links, source visibility, privacy boundaries, and provenance should remain understandable and controllable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Body text contrast must meet 4.5:1; large text must meet 3:1.
- Complete keyboard navigation and visible focus states are required.
- All meaningful visuals need text equivalents or useful labels.
- Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; content must remain visible without animation.
- Layout and typography must remain usable at 200% zoom and on narrow mobile screens.
